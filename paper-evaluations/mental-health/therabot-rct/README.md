# Therabot RCT - Clinical Evaluation
## Paper
Randomized Clinical Trial of a Generative AI Chatbot-therabot for Mental Health disorder Treatment
## Publication
NEJM AI, 2025
## Clinical Question
Can a generative AI chatbot provide clinically meaningful support for adults with depression(MDD), anxiety(GAD), and clinically high-risk feeding and eating disorders(CHR-FED)?
## 1. Clinical Problem and Rationale
The global burden and prevalence of Mental health disorders is rising steeply and is further exacerbated by shortages in the mental health workforce, continuing stigma attached to seeking timely therapy. Clinically grounded, evidence based, accessible and appropriately regulated AI applications could potentially help bridge gaps in this area and improce access, and ensure continuity in providing clinically impactful care for patients. However the significance of such AI applications should be determined not only by user enagagement but also by clincial validity,long term impact and safety.
## 2. Study Design

**Study type:** Prospective, Randomized controlled trial

**Sample size:** A total number of 210 participants. Participants were first stratified into three symptom groups with MDD, GAD, and CHR-FED and then randomly assigned within each group to either the Therabot intervention or waitlist control.

**Allocation:** Participants were distributed across the MDD, GAD, and CHR-FED symptom groups using a 1:1:1 allocation ratio. Within each symptom group, participants were randomized to Therabot or waitlist control.

**Population:** Adults who screened positive for clinically significant symptoms of major depressive disorder (MDD), generalized anxiety disorder (GAD), or clinically high-risk feeding and eating disorders (CHR-FED), based on self-reported baseline questionnaires. Participants represented varied age, education, and gender groups. Individuals with active suicidality, mania, or psychosis were excluded.

- **Intervention:** Participants randomized to Therabot were prompted daily to interact with the generative AI chatbot during the 4-week treatment phase. Therabot provided empathetic responses, emotional validation, targeted therapeutic interventions, and questions prompting further elaboration.

- **Comparator:** Waitlist control. Participants assigned to the control condition did not have access to Therabot during the initial study period and were subsequently given access.

- **Follow-up:** Outcomes were assessed at baseline, 4 weeks (post-intervention), and 8 weeks (follow-up).

- **Primary clinical outcomes:** Changes in depression, anxiety, and weight-concern symptoms from baseline, measured using:
  - **PHQ-9** — depression symptoms
  - **GAD-Q-IV** — anxiety symptoms
  - **Weight Concerns Scale (WCS)** — weight concerns associated with CHR-FED
  - 
    ### Secondary Outcomes
    Assessed **user engagement, acceptability, usability, satisfaction, and therapeutic alliance** with Therabot.

- **User engagement** — Frequency and extent of interaction with Therabot
- **Usability** — Ease of use and user experience
- **Satisfaction** — Participant satisfaction with the intervention
- **Therapeutic alliance** — Perceived quality of the therapeutic relationship, measured using the **WAI-SR**

### Clinical Evaluation Consideration

The study assessed both **clinical symptom change and user experience**. High engagement, satisfaction, and therapeutic alliance indicate acceptability and perceived usefulness but do not independently establish clinical efficacy. Clinical benefit should therefore be evaluated primarily through changes in validated clinical outcome measures.

## 4. Key Findings

### Clinical Outcomes

- **Depression:** Therabot was associated with a significant reduction in PHQ-9 scores compared with the waitlist control.
- **Anxiety:** Therabot was associated with a significant reduction in GAD-Q-IV scores compared with the waitlist control.
- **CHR-FED:** Therabot was associated with a significant reduction in Weight Concerns Scale (WCS) scores compared with the waitlist control.
- Improvements were observed following the **4-week intervention** and were assessed again at **8-week follow-up**.

### Engagement and Therapeutic Outcomes

- Participants demonstrated **high engagement** with Therabot during the intervention period.
- Participants reported favorable **usability and satisfaction** with the intervention.
- **Therapeutic alliance** with Therabot was reported at levels comparable to those observed in some human therapist–patient relationships.

### Overall Observation

The trial provides evidence of **short-term symptom improvement and high user acceptability** of Therabot compared with a waitlist control.

However, the findings demonstrate efficacy relative to **no intervention**, rather than superiority or equivalence to established mental-health treatments.

- **Secondary outcomes:** User engagement, acceptability, usability, satisfaction, and therapeutic alliance.
